## About Me

My name is Yu Duan (You-Dwan, in Chinese: 段昱). I joined MIT EECS as a PhD student in 2023, and I work with Prof. Kanaka Rajan (Kempner Institute, Harvard) on machine-learning models of the brain. In summer 2026, I was a research scientist intern at Adobe, where I worked on post-training and efficient sampling for image-editing generative models. As an undergrad, I studied computer science at the Institute for Interdisciplinary Information Sciences at Tsinghua University, where I worked with Prof. Kaisheng Ma and Prof. Yi Zhong on networks with synaptic plasticity and models of the fruit fly mushroom body. I was also a visiting student in Brain and Cognitive Sciences at MIT, where I worked with Prof. Robert Yang on multi-system models of working memory.

My PhD research focuses on modeling neural dynamics: I build machine-learning models that can act as working surrogates of neural circuits. These include foundation models trained across many sessions, animals and species that forecast neural population activity and adapt quickly to new recordings; models that predict how circuits respond to causal perturbations such as optogenetic stimulation, which could serve as in-silico testbeds for designing stimulation and closed-loop control; and generative models that capture the full distribution of possible futures of stochastic neural dynamics.

In my free time, I enjoy playing video games, hiking / skiing in the New England forests. I’m a fan of both rock and classical music.
